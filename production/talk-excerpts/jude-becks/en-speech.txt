On my first day as captain, I thought my job was to have every answer. I talked a lot. I corrected every detail. And something unexpected happened: my teammates stopped talking.

We had talent, but everyone had a different idea of what helping meant. For one person, it meant solving everything alone. For another, never disagreeing. For someone else, waiting for instructions. We could wear the same shirt and still be playing different games.

One day we changed the meeting. Instead of asking who had failed, we asked what each person needed from the others to do their job well. The answers were small and specific: tell me before you change the play; let me know when you cannot keep up; correct me without embarrassing me.

We turned those requests into three agreements. Flag a task when it is at risk. Confirm who will receive the next pass. And set aside a few minutes to review what happened, even after a win.

Mistakes did not disappear. Our response changed. Asking for help stopped feeling like weakness, because everyone knew what to do when someone asked.

Think about your team. When you say commitment, quality or trust, are you describing the same behaviour? Choose one of those words and finish this sentence together: tomorrow, people will see it when we... Write down an action anyone can observe.

Team culture begins there: in what we do when nobody is applauding.
