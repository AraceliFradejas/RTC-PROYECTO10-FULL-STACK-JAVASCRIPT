When the game gets tough, we often ask our team to run faster. More meetings. More reports. More urgency. But what if speed isn't the problem? What if everyone is running towards a different goal?

At a mobility cooperative, we introduced a new tool for handling customer issues. On paper, it was a major step forward. In practice, the same enquiry still travelled from one desk to the next. We had changed the screen, but not the way we made decisions.

One morning, a colleague asked a simple question: “Who can tell the customer what we're going to do, without asking three people for permission?” Nobody answered. That was the problem.

Before adding another feature, we brought the team together. We agreed what each person could resolve, when they needed help, and who would own the next step. Then we tested that agreement on just one type of enquiry. We listened to what wasn't working and improved it together.

For example, imagine a request sitting unanswered because nobody knows whether they can offer an alternative. The change might be as simple as agreeing which options the person helping the customer can suggest, and when they should ask for advice. For one week, we observe what happens: does the customer receive a useful answer, does the team understand the agreement, and where do questions arise? Then we discuss those cases together. That gives us a concrete experience to improve the process, instead of another assumption.

That's innovation too. Sometimes, progress doesn't begin with new technology. It begins with clear responsibility.

Tomorrow, before asking people to work harder, try three things: ask where the work gets stuck; agree on one decision the team can make; and set a time to review what you've learned.

You don't have to win the whole game today. You need to know what the next inch is, who will cover it, and how you'll support each other.
